# Human-flag reference audit

**Status: research only. No flag pose group is accepted by this note.** The current draft has 16 flag exercises: eight clutch-flag stages and eight press-flag stages. The publisher confirms that the Kavadlos' human-flag chapter contains 25 progressive drills, including clutch and support-press stages, but its freely inspectable material does not expose all 16 CC2 stage photos.

## What the accessible references establish

- [Dragon Door's Street Workout listing](https://direct.dragondoor.com/products/eb87) identifies Al and Danny Kavadlo as authors and says Chapter 11 progresses through 25 human-flag drills, naming Side Plank, Clutch Flag, Support Press, Vertical Flag, Crucifix, One Arm Flag, and Human Flag. This confirms the broader progression, not the precise geometry of every CC2 step.
- [Dragon Door's catalog](https://www.dragondoor.com/assets/1/15/HS2018SPRING-4.pdf) independently identifies the book and the same chapter-level scope. It is a publisher catalog, not an exercise-photo source.
- In [Al Kavadlo's Clutch Flag article](https://breakingmuscle.com/strength-conditioning-al-kavadlo-week-2-day-2/), he describes the clutch as an easier route to the full press flag: the torso helps squeeze the pole, the body is shorter, and the skill teaches alignment. He also cautions about inner-arm chafing. This supports the broad distinction between clutch and press grips, not the eight-step breakdown.
- Al Kavadlo's [human-flag tutorial with GMB](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1omDSunail0) is an inspectable primary demonstration of a short progression: support press, chamber hold, vertical flag, one-leg-tucked flag, and full flag (approximately 0:19–1:01). It does not show all the CC2 press steps or the clutch sequence.
- In Al Kavadlo's [written human-flag tutorial](https://archive.t-nation.com/training/how-to-perform-the-human-flag/), the usual press grip is top hand overhand and bottom hand supinated; the lower arm pushes and upper arm pulls. He recommends a near-vertical body first, then lowering from the top, with bent or straddled legs as leverage options. This independently supports the broad press mechanics, not CC2's exact stage photos.
- Grace Kavadlo's [human-flag tutorial](https://dieselgrace.wordpress.com/2017/08/25/learn-to-do-the-human-flag/) likewise cues bottom-arm push/top-arm pull, straight arms, a tucked flag, then extending one leg while lowering toward horizontal. It is a second instructor demonstration, not an authoritative source for the named CC2 stages.
- [Hybrid Calisthenics' clutch-grip instructions](https://www.hybridcalisthenics.com/clutch-flag-grip) give a useful, inspectable grip reference: upper arm reaches behind the back armpit with fingertips down and hand near sternum height; the opposite elbow wedges into the abdomen and that hand grips near hip height; the lower body stays behind the pole. It recommends straightening the torso gradually, with knees allowed to remain bent. Treat this as corroboration for practical clutch geometry, not as a substitute for Al Kavadlo's exact CC2 method.

## Geometry for review against contract and available references

The exercise names and ordering below are the working contract's 16 stages, not a claim that an online source has visually verified each one.

**Clutch sequence:** Clutch Hang → Diagonal Split Clutch → Diagonal Tuck Clutch → Diagonal Clutch → Horizontal Tuck Clutch → Horizontal Split Clutch → Bent Leg Clutch Flag → Clutch Flag. The distinguishing feature to check in the photos is torso contact with the pole: the lower hand pushes near the waist while the upper arm/forearm and torso clamp the pole. As the sequence advances, the body moves from hanging to diagonal and then horizontal; tucked, split, bent-leg, and straight-leg shapes should remain distinct. A complete clutch flag uses a straight horizontal body; follow the contract’s side-on grip and avoid turning the belly upward. Exact torso orientation is not photo-verified here.

**Press sequence:** Support Press → Press Hang → Kick Press → Vertical Chamber Press → Vertical Press → Split Press Flag → Bent Leg Press Flag → Press Flag. The lower arm pushes and the upper arm pulls. The support press is a wall/bar support drill; it is not a pole hold. Press Hang remains a hanging position, while Kick Press is dynamic. The chamber is a bent-knee, rising position; Vertical Press is the straight vertical position. In Split Press Flag, the contract specifies that the leg nearest the pole bends at the knee and comes forward while the other leg extends. Bent Leg Press Flag and full Press Flag should remain separate horizontal targets, with the latter's legs straight.

## Geometry notes for the Blender review · 2026-10-15

The contract's 16 IDs are eight clutch stages (`flag-clutch-hang` through `flag-clutch`) and eight press stages (`flag-support-press` through `flag-press`). The contract already records the author's broad two-method distinction: clutch uses torso/pole contact; press uses straight-arm push/pull. The public references support these general cues and do not establish the exact poses of every named stage.

- **Clutch grip detail to check:** the contract's “wrap that arm around” and lower elbow braced above the hip are directionally consistent with the instructor references. The public clutch-grip tutorial adds an upper hand behind the back armpit, fingertips down, roughly sternum height; opposite elbow wedged at the abdominals; lower hand near hip; and lower body behind the pole. These are good pose-review landmarks, but do not label them verified CC2 photo details. Keep torso contact and the braced lower elbow visible through each clutch pose. The public sources do not settle the exact hand/wrist orientation for every CC2 stage.
- **Clutch stage separation:** keep Diagonal Split (one leg tucked, one extended) visibly distinct from Diagonal Tuck (both knees tucked) and Diagonal Clutch (legs straight together). At horizontal, keep Horizontal Tuck distinct from Horizontal Split and Bent-Leg Clutch; the full Clutch Flag has straight legs together. This follows the contract text, not confirmed book photos. Avoid asserting a specific belly/chest-facing angle as source-verified; the article confirms body/pole contact but not all stage orientations.
- **Press mechanics:** keep the pole-only stages distinct from Support Press, whose contract explicitly uses an overhead bar and adjacent fixed wall/support. In pole poses, show a separated top/bottom grip, lower arm straight and pressing, upper arm straight and pulling. Kavadlo's public tutorial supports the common top-overhand/bottom-supinated grip; the contract's phrase “take the press grip” does not itself specify wrist orientation, so this is a reference-informed interpretation, not an exact CC2 check.
- **Press progression and split:** a vertical or tucked body is a shorter-lever position; the public references support gradual lowering and leg extension as broad progressions. Keep Kick Press visibly dynamic, Vertical Chamber tucked, Vertical Press straight, and horizontal targets separated. The contract specifies the pole-near knee at roughly 90 degrees for Split Press; show that knee forward and the far leg straight. Confirm the pole side against the actual left/right pose before mirroring.
