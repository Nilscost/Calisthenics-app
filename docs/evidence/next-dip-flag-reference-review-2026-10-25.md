# Bounded video reference review: Russian dip and flag kick press

I inspected sampled contact sheets made from three source videos per movement. Samples are roughly one second apart for the shorter excerpts and two to four seconds apart for longer tutorials. These are selected-frame observations, not complete playback or expert form approval. Timecodes below are absolute source-video times; downloaded files in this folder are trimmed excerpts unless stated otherwise.

## Russian dip

1. [Adam Frater, “How to do Russian Dips (Full Tutorial)”](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vFEVeEcRot8), **380–477 s** (97 s, “Adding it All Together”). The performer demonstrates parallel-bar support, lowers into a deep bent-arm position with the upper arm/forearm close to the bars, rocks back to a low supported position, then drives the shoulders forward and presses back toward support. The camera is a close, oblique outdoor view; the bar and torso obscure some elbow/forearm contacts, and the excerpt also includes explanation and resets. No spotter is visible in sampled frames. This is the clearest sampled full-sequence reference, but samples do not resolve exact contact anatomy or continuity between every phase.

2. [TrainFTW, “Russian Dips”](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=og3Txbm8-LQ), **0–50 s** (full short tutorial). The athlete works between tall wooden jerk blocks/boxes, repeatedly moving through the low position and pressing back up; text overlays describe forearms resting on the apparatus and rocking the shoulders forward to press. The front-facing wide shot keeps the body and both supports visible, but text covers part of the right side and the bulky supports hide some arm contact. This is a block-supported variation, not clearly the target parallel-bar setup; no spotter is visible.

3. [JustCalisthenics, “How to Russian (elbow) dips? | Russian(elbow) dips tutorial”](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Eg402ejbHxU), **116–180 s** (“Transition practice”). In sampled frames, the athlete uses a red outdoor bar and rocks between upper support and forearm/elbow support. His feet remain on the ground and appear to assist the back-and-forward drill, so this is a transition practice rather than an unassisted full repetition. The side view shows the trunk and bar reasonably well, though overlays and the bar obscure exact arm contact at some moments.

The clips show related but non-identical low positions: parallel-bar upper-arm/forearm support, box-supported forearm rest, and a feet-assisted transition drill. They support inspecting a controlled shoulder shift into and out of the low support, but do not establish a single precise elbow or forearm contact point. The target catalog instruction begins at dip bottom, rolls back to forearms, rolls forward to hands, then presses; sampled sources do not directly verify that exact variant.

## Flag kick press

1. [TrainingPal, “How to do Press To Human Flag - Tutorial / Progressions”](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=O-8NIJS-05Q), **33–70 s**. The excerpt starts with an inclined/low flag hold, shows a separate tucked-flag variation, then at about 50–64 s shows a kick-up sequence progressing from an angled body to a near-horizontal flag; around 65–70 s it transitions into a vertical/inverted position. The pole and both hands remain visible, and the athlete is not visibly assisted in these samples. This is an edited progression montage with named stages, not an uninterrupted single repetition, and the brief kick-up segment does not establish the catalog’s precise hip-over-lower-hand action or soft landing.

2. [Andry Strong, “How to Do a Human Flag | Calisthenics Tutorial”](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y4Vwwz8vV2U), **164–245 s** (chaptered “Learning to kick up” and “Kick to vertical”). The instructor demonstrates repeated kick-up attempts at a wall-mounted stall-bar structure, with feet returning to the floor between attempts; sampled frames show an angled body moving toward an inverted/vertical position. The wide side/three-quarter view shows most of the body, but the wall structure partly hides the far-side arm and torso. No spotter or band is visible in the sampled interval. This is a kick-up progression, not a clearly isolated catalog-style kick-press rep; it does not show a controlled landing in the reviewed interval.

3. [Strength Project, “Human Flag Pole tutorial - How to do a flagpole training bar hold”](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VA4l6FIOMyI), **0–152 s** (full tutorial, sampled every four seconds). This is a mixed tutorial rather than one kick-press sequence. It includes instruction and several setups; a helper visibly assists a trainee during some flag positions around 56–84 s, while later material shows other performers and ground-supported drills. People, apparatus, and edits obscure some body/pole contacts. The source is useful for identifying assistance and variant differences, but not as a clean unassisted target-repetition comparison.

These samples cover kick-ups toward vertical or horizontal positions, assisted holds, and changing progressions. They are not interchangeable. The catalog asks for an explosive two-leg kick, hips briefly over the lower hand, then a soft landing without a hold. The reviewed excerpts do not consistently show that complete sequence, and none supports a numerical form threshold. Visual review of the rendered target clip remains necessary.
