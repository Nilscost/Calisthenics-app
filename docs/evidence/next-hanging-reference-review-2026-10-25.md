# Hanging-core reference frame review

I used `/tmp/calisthenics-pose-venv/bin/python` with `yt_dlp` to retrieve public videos into `media/`, then extracted time-labelled contact sheets with OpenCV. I inspected the sheets with `view_image`. The downloaded source metadata, hashes, attempts and extraction products are local under this directory. The initial `best[height<=480]` format selector failed with “Requested format is not available”; the corrected split-stream selector retrieved all nine clips. No catalog/app media was modified.

These are selected-frame observations at 1 fps (2 fps for some longer intervals), not full-speed playback or a frame-by-frame review. Contact sheets can miss short contacts and conceal motion between samples. They support variant identification and broad sequence questions; they do not approve exercise form.

## Files, sampling, and limitations

Original video files and download attempt logs are in `media/`; time-labelled contact sheets are in `frames/`. The sheets document the intervals above. Sources were downloaded after a selector correction (`bestvideo[height<=480]+bestaudio/...`); the first attempt failed because a combined-format selector matched no available format. All media are local analysis copies, not app assets.

I inspected still frames only, not continuous full-speed playback. Exact toe/heel contact, force, hidden-side alignment, assisted load, and movement between samples remain uncertain. The three heel-hook clips all use Army climbing-drill instruction and include spotters; that shared context should not be generalized into a universal calisthenics standard. The dragon-flag references mix full negatives, hold, tuck/one-leg regressions and full repetitions, and none of the selected intervals demonstrates the catalog’s entire negative-then-tuck-roll sequence in a clearly uncut, unobstructed view. No form approval is implied.


Original reference clips and sampled sheets are retained only in local work analysis; they are not bundled or redistributed with the app.
