# R8: breadth without a crowded training path

**Status: design review only. No content or behavior is approved by this note.** This review challenges [the progression discussion](27-progression-review.md) against the generated catalog and current app flows.

## Findings, highest impact first

1. **The new breadth can spill into default workouts.** The catalog has 533 entries, 517 outside the 16 routine-only Army drills. The visible Core, Pull, and Push trees alone contain 93, 51, and 47 exercises. Progress has a tab for each broad family group, and search returns up to 12 alphabetical matches. More significantly, default session sizing adds eligible fillers until it reaches 30 minutes, up to 12 exercises per round. The filler pool is profile- and prerequisite-filtered and selects one lowest-rank exercise per family, but the 379 additions still change what an ordinary workout can include. **Proposal:** keep a small, stable default set of movement families; make extra families an explicit “add more” choice. Do not let new catalog rows silently expand the familiar workout.

2. **The app has two different meanings of “next.”** Mastery uses each policy's explicit `mainSuccessorId`; the rep-range stepper instead sorts every usable member of a family by `difficultyRank`. A family rank is a useful browse hint, but it is not a reviewed path. In the current push-up family, the imported One-Arm Wall Push-Away is rank 12, between high incline/incline work and the knee push-up (rank 20); a range step can therefore surface it as the next choice even though the original incline policy points to Standard Push-Up. **Proposal:** make “next” follow the explicit main successor. Put alternatives and specializations in a separate chooser, clearly labeled as optional. If the stepper is intentionally a family browser, label it as such and stop calling it a progression stepper.

3. **Appending every source rung as a visible progression edge still creates clutter.** The importer preserves legacy edges and adds all contract `insertAfter` edges; the review report flags 47 parent rows for judgment, including 19 lower-rank insertion candidates. A tree that draws every edge treats support drills, alternate techniques, and candidates for the main line with equal visual weight. Only the feet-elevated/diamond pair is currently an owner-confirmed comparable group. **Proposal:** give each family one reviewed primary ladder. Show alternate paths below it as “other ways to train this movement,” and show prep/support work as recommendations rather than required rungs. Keep `routineOnly` hidden from browsing and general auto-fill, as it is now; fixed programs can still name their drills.

4. **Equipment and partial-range steps are poor universal gates.** An elevated pistol or a wall/box-assisted push-up may be a useful route for some people, but access to that prop is not evidence of readiness and lack of it should not block a bodyweight route. The planner filters an active exercise by profile, then can fall back to the routine's preferred variation if the active step is unavailable; that fallback is not the same as continuing through an explicit alternative route. Likewise, a partial pull-up or half one-arm push-up can support training without being a prerequisite to full-range strength. The current importer turns `insertAfter` and `alsoRequires` into conjunctive tier-5 app gates. **Proposal:** keep equipment-specific and partial-ROM work optional unless a source explicitly establishes it as a necessary step. For any proposed gate, state whether it means “all routes require this” or “this route requires this”; do not infer that from rank or adjacency.

5. **Programs are a good curated front door, but their list is dense.** The eight fixed programs preserve exact order and targets, and an unsupported equipment profile disables Start with the missing item named; they do not borrow level ladders or substitutions. The screen also expands every prescribed item and a flow switch in all eight cards. Pilates Intermediate alone has 23 items. **Proposal:** keep all eight programs, but make the list a compact title, duration, and equipment summary; open one program to see its exact sequence and flow setting. Keep each program's authored flow default and make overrides discoverable in its detail view.

## Choices to settle before changing routes

- Should the rep-range stepper mean “next main step” (recommended for coherence with mastery), or remain a difficulty-sorted family browser with a different label?
- Which small set of families is eligible for automatic time-filling, and should any newly added family enter that set without a separate review?
- For each apparent intermediate, is it a main-line prerequisite, an alternate route, or optional supporting work? Decide this explicitly for partial pull-ups, elevated pistols, half-range one-arm push-ups, and other equipment- or range-specific variants.
- Should visible progression trees emphasize one primary path and reveal optional branches on demand, while search remains the comprehensive library?

The useful product split is: **a complete library to browse, a small number of explicit main paths to master, and optional variations/support work that never become mandatory merely because they have a lower rank or an `insertAfter` link.**

## Concrete route audit

The classifications below are proposals for review, not approved catalog edits. “Legacy” refers to a relationship already present in the R7 starter catalog; “R8 draft” refers to an imported exercise or edge in the current contract. The current generated catalog has 533 variations across the extended tabs, but most additions should widen the library rather than lengthen a single mandatory ladder.

| Priority | Evidence in current catalog | Proposed classification | Why it deserves review |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Bird Dog → prone Swimmer** is the current main successor (`bird-dog` policy); the R8 draft adds a distinct `bird-dog-quadruped → arm raise → leg raise → bird-dog` ramp and `quadraplex` as a child of Bird Dog. | Keep the quadruped ramp as the **main step**; treat prone Swimmer as a **supporting drill** or separate prone-core route, and decide whether Quadraplex is an **alternate** or a named goal. | The main edge changes from quadruped to prone, while the new, lower-load quadruped stages form a coherent same-position progression. This appears to conflate two useful routes rather than make either exercise invalid. The old Bird Dog → Swimmer edge is legacy; the staged quadruped ladder is R8 draft. |
| 2 | **Advanced Tuck Front Lever Row → Ice Cream Maker** is the current main successor. The R8 draft also inserts `Straddle Front Lever Row → Hang to Front Lever Row → Full Front Lever Row` after the same advanced-tuck row. | Review **Advanced Tuck → Straddle → Full** as the candidate **main path**; keep Hang to Row and Ice Cream Maker as **transition specializations**, with comparability still to establish. | Straddle and full rows change the lever length. Ice Cream Maker and Hang to Row add transitions; neither is automatically a mandatory rung or a same-level equivalent. Review the lever-length route separately from these useful specialties. The mixed successor is in the current generated policy; the new straddle-to-full path is R8 draft. |
| 3 | **Front Plank → Hollow Tuck** is the legacy main route. R8 adds `Long-Lever Plank → Body Saw` and `Contralateral Plank → Arm-Leg Reach` as separate branches. | Preserve Hollow Tuck as an **alternate core route**; present Long-Lever and Contralateral as separate **supporting/advanced plank branches**, not extra mandatory rungs. | These are different progressions of trunk leverage and limb control. A single rank-ordered “next” list would imply that every plank trainee should switch among them. The Hollow Tuck edge predates R8; the two plank-specific branches are R8 draft. |
| 4 | **Lying Leg Raise → V-Up** is the legacy main route. R8 adds `Tuck V-Up → Straddle V-Up` as another child/continuation around the same starting point. | Keep standard V-Up as the **main step** and expose tuck/straddle as an **alternate route**. | A tuck can reduce the lever while straddle changes the leg position; neither needs to become a universal gate before a standard V-Up. The current contract makes the tuck-to-straddle sequence explicit, so it can remain discoverable without displacing the legacy main route. |

These entries are the strongest route-shape concerns found in the reviewed families; many other R8 additions are coherent as **same-level alternatives** or specialty work. Examples include Commando/Kipping/Jumping Pullover beneath Pull-Up; Cossack Squat, Wall Sit, and Horse Stance beneath Air Squat; and elevated/negative/counterbalance pistol variants. They broaden the library but change grip, range, balance, or training emphasis, so they should not become serial prerequisites. Likewise, the six capped explosive push-up variants are power alternatives, while Diamond’s Uneven and Pseudo-Planche branches are specializations; the owner-confirmed Feet-Elevated/Diamond exercise group remains an explicit **same-level alternate** only.

The R8 import's `insertAfter` edge should therefore mean “related route entry,” not “must precede every successor.” Review each edge for one of four display roles—**main step, same-level alternate, supporting drill, or program-only**—before using it to gate a route or drive a single “next” action. Keep the 16 `routineOnly` Army drills in the last category; their inclusion in fixed programs does not require exposing them in browse results or auto-fill.

## Pose review: breadth must also mean teachable movements

The pose review adds a practical filter to the route decisions above. A source that is ready for **draft Blender rendering** is not yet an accepted instructional movie. The current mannequin has no separate scapula, cervical retraction, head yaw/sidebend or individual finger joints. Do not turn that technical coverage into a claim that every catalog instruction is demonstrated.

- **Seated chin tuck** currently has the correct seated setup but no retraction action. It needs another demonstration or a richer rig before inclusion as a taught movement; a static movie would be misleading.
- **Scapular wall/knee/full push-ups and support shrugs** retain correct supports and straight elbows, but their blade motion is only a tiny thorax approximation. These deserve an explicit teaching-quality review even if the geometry checks pass. They are optional preparation/support work, not extra compulsory rungs in the push-up or dip ladder.
- **Palm pulses and finger lifts** can show the rigid-hand palm pivot, but cannot show individual finger lifts. Either demonstrate the full instruction with another asset or review a narrower content description; do not silently treat partial coverage as complete.
- **Supported neck nods and chin-tuck head lifts** have limited pitch/lift coverage. The independent tuck and fully supported nod are not separately articulated. Four-way isometric hand placement is visible, but resistance itself remains schematic.
- The reported **thoracic-rotation video problem remains open**. The same lack of independent spinal rotation can affect new twisting demonstrations. Review what a person would actually learn from the movie, rather than accepting a whole-body turn as proof of isolated thoracic motion.

This strengthens the proposal to keep preparation and specialist work available through deliberate choices, with a small default training pool. A broad library is useful when the movement, instruction, demonstration and role in training agree. Availability alone should not create a progression edge or a default-workout slot. These are review findings, not implemented exclusions or approved wording changes.
